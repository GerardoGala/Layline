Layline Strategy 101

Knowing when to tack for an efficient approach to the Windward Mark

What is a Layline?

A layline is the course sailed on a tack that will take your boat directly to the Windward Mark.

The goal is not simply to sail toward the mark. The goal is to decide when to tack so that your new tack takes you directly to the mark.

The Basic Geometry

An ILCA cannot sail directly into the wind. When sailing upwind, the boat must sail at an angle to the wind on either a port tack or starboard tack.

  • The port-tack layline is the course from which a tack will put you directly toward the Windward Mark.
  • The starboard-tack layline works the same way from the opposite side.
  • The correct layline depends on the wind direction and the boat's upwind sailing angle.

Three Layline Decisions

1. Before the Layline — Keep Sailing

If your boat is still short of the layline, a tack will leave you short of the mark. Continue sailing on your current tack until the appropriate layline is reached.

2. On the Layline — Tack

When you reach the layline, tack. Your new tack should put the boat on a direct course toward the Windward Mark.

3. Beyond the Layline — Too Late

If you sail beyond the layline before tacking, your new tack will take you past the mark. You have sailed too far on the current tack.

The Golden Layline Rule

Tack when your new tack will take you directly to the mark.

What Should You Be Thinking?

  1. Where is the Windward Mark?
  2. What is the wind direction?
  3. Am I still short of the layline?
  4. If I tack now, will my new course take me directly to the mark?
  5. If not, keep sailing.
